# Visual system

## Hierarchy

Read the image in this order:

1. subject silhouette and relationship;
2. action, pose, gaze, or contact;
3. black mass versus restrained accent color;
4. sparse face and identity cues;
5. one to three environmental anchors.

If a detail does not help one of these five levels, remove it.

## Line

- Medium-light rather than hairline-thin or poster-bold.
- Natural micro-wobble from hand movement; unevenness varies by stroke and is not applied as a global noise effect.
- Mild pressure change within a stroke.
- Slightly blunt or relaxed terminals.
- Long contours may contain one gentle bow or kink.
- Joins may miss or overlap slightly, but anatomy and object identity must remain readable.
- Use one committed contour, not repeated exploratory sketch lines.

## Faces

Keep adult proportions and the real viewing angle. Simplify, do not genericize.

- Brows: short, incomplete, not perfectly paired.
- Eyes: short upper curve or short horizontal mark plus a small pupil. Omit lower lids and enclosed almond shapes.
- Nose: one hook, tiny angle, or nostril cue; never model the full bridge and nostrils.
- Mouth: short line; optional tiny lower-lip cue. Avoid glossy lips or full lip contour.
- Omit skin shading, blush, eyelashes, realistic iris detail, and symmetrical beauty rendering.
- Preserve expression through eye direction, brow tilt, mouth angle, cheek contour, and head tilt—not extra detail.

## Hair

- Establish the hairstyle silhouette before internal strands.
- Dark hair: one dominant solid mass plus a small number of broad irregular white channels.
- Light, dyed, or highlighted hair: preserve the real base color and the placement logic of dyed/highlighted areas. Simplify to base + one or two secondary colors.
- White channels describe strand separation; they do not replace real dyed color.
- Do not render hundreds of strands, gradients, gloss, or photographic highlights.

## Fills and palette

- Black is solid, opaque, and calm.
- Colored regions are clean flat shapes. Their edges may be very slightly hand-shaped; interiors remain quiet.
- Do not simulate broad marker passes. The approved full-body reference intentionally uses clean flat color rather than visible surface strokes.
- Do not add bubbles, holes, mottling, cloudy density, paper texture, colored-pencil grain, watercolor blooms, hatching, or airbrushed shading.
- Use photo-derived colors. Default reduction: black/white/gray plus one dominant accent and optionally one supporting muted color.
- Preserve a distinctive original color when it carries identity: dyed hair, a signature garment, pet markings, or a key prop.

## Composition and space

- Prefer a white or near-white field with substantial breathing room.
- Preserve the source's meaningful direction and balance, but crop or reposition when needed for a clearer illustration.
- Delete full photographic backgrounds. Retain only a few anchors such as a floor line, chair edge, bed shape, window line, rock contour, horizon, table plane, or wind turbine silhouette.
- Do not turn anchors into a complete environment.

## Stable versus optional features

Stable: sparse reconstruction, white space, black line, organic wobble, solid black mass, restrained flat color, simplified face, preserved relationship.

Optional and photo-dependent: accent hue, amount of black mass, environmental anchors, decorative motion marks, hair channels, and small props.

Avoid treating incidental motifs from a reference—stars, handwritten words, food icons, a particular outfit, or a specific pose—as mandatory style features.
